# Mathematical and Computational Modeling in Behavior Science {.unnumbered}

**A Systematic Guide to Building, Evaluating, and Applying Formal Models of Behavior**

*Prepared for doctoral students in behavior science*

# Preface {.unnumbered}

This handbook accompanies a doctoral course in mathematical and computational modeling designed specifically for students in behavior science. The course exists because of a persistent gap: behavior science has a rich tradition of quantitative thinking, yet many of its doctoral graduates receive little formal training in the logic of model construction, evaluation, and comparison. Herrnstein's matching law, Mazur's hyperbolic discounting function, Hursh's demand equation -- these are among the most successful quantitative models in all of psychology. But knowing that these models exist is not the same as knowing how they were built, why they take the forms they do, how to evaluate them against alternatives, or how to construct new models when existing ones fall short. This course, and this handbook, aim to close that gap.

The backbone of the handbook is an 8-step modeling framework adapted from engineering and the physical sciences. Every chapter applies the framework, from simple linear models of cumulative responding to dynamical systems and machine learning. By the time you finish the course, the framework should function less as a checklist and more as a habit of thought.

The handbook is organized into four parts. Part I provides the foundations: why modeling matters, how the 8-step framework works, and practical skills for reading, building, and critiquing models. Part II follows the 13-week course sequence, with each chapter corresponding to one week. Part III contains practice problems with worked solutions and a catalog of common pitfalls. Part IV includes reference materials: key equations, a glossary, and quick-reference sheets.

A word about expectations. The goal of this course is fluency, not mastery of pure mathematics. Fluency means you can read a model and understand what it claims, evaluate whether its assumptions are reasonable, build a simple model, fit it to data, compare it to alternatives, and communicate the results. You will not emerge as a mathematician. You will emerge as a behavior scientist who is comfortable with formal models.

# How to Use This Handbook {.unnumbered}

This handbook is designed to be read sequentially on first pass and used as a reference thereafter. The material builds cumulatively: concepts introduced in early chapters are assumed in later ones, and the 8-step modeling framework threads through every chapter.

**Part I: Foundations** (the modeling framework) provides the conceptual groundwork. Read it before the first week of class.

**Part II: Weekly Course Guide** follows the 13-week course sequence. Each chapter includes core concepts, a detailed application of the 8-step framework, worked examples, and reflection prompts. Read each chapter before the corresponding class session.

**Part III: Practice and Application** contains practice problems organized by chapter, each with a fully worked solution. Work the problems after each class session.

**Part IV: Appendices** contains reference materials. Consult as needed.

Read actively. When you encounter an equation, stop and ask: What does each term represent? What are its units? What happens when a parameter increases? The 8-step framework is applied in every chapter. This repetition is deliberate -- by the end of the course, it should be internalized.
